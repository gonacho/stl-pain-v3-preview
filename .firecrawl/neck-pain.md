## Common Causes of Neck Pain

Understanding the potential causes of neck pain can help you prevent it in the future. For those already experiencing neck problems, it can be helpful to understand the causes in order to identify what may have caused your condition.

Neck pain is often the result of one of a variety of factors:

Poor Posture

Poor posture is often a result of long periods of desk work or from staring down at devices for prolonged periods. In recent years, posture issues have become a leading cause of neck pain.

Muscle Strain

Frequent strain or overuse of neck muscles increases the chance of causing neck pain.

Sleeping Position

Improper sleeping positions or sleeping on an unsupportive pillow can cause neck injuries to develop, resulting in chronic pain.

Degenerative Conditions

Degenerative conditions like Arthritis can develop as people age. This causes the cushioning in the neck to wear down and cause discomfort and mobility issues.

Pinched Nerves

Pinched nerves occur when the vertebrae in your neck compress or you are suffering from a herniated disc.

Stress

A typical stress response involves tightening the muscles in the neck and shoulders. The tension this causes can lead to neck pain.

Injury

A whiplash injury or a sports injury can cause severe neck pain that becomes reoccurring if not treated properly.

## FAQs About Neck Pain

What is the most common reason for neck pain?

While there isn’t one definitive cause of neck pain, today stress and poor posture are the most common causes. Poor posture, like hunching over a laptop at work, and stress can cause muscle tightness and other conditions that can lead to neck pain.

How do I know if my neck pain is serious?

Experiencing higher levels of pain or symptoms that have a major impact on your life are typical indicators of a more serious condition. Taking action sooner than later can help to minimize the impact and severity of your condition on your life.

What treatment is best for neck pain?

Visiting a pain management clinic is often the best treatment for chronic neck pain. A good provider can identify the underlying cause of your pain and work with you to treat the problem. Even after treating it, they can help you understand why the pain started in the first place so you can avoid repeat problems.

Does neck pain ever go away?

Minor instances of neck pain will usually go away on their own. In more severe cases of neck pain, though, medical care will likely be required.
