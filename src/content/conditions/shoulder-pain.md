## Understanding Shoulder Pain

**The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body, which makes it uniquely vulnerable to injury and degeneration.** It relies on a network of muscles, tendons, ligaments, and a shallow ball-and-socket design that trades stability for range of motion.

This design means the shoulder depends heavily on soft tissue for support. When any component of that system is damaged, the entire joint suffers. Pain may originate from the **rotator cuff**, the **bursa**, the **labrum**, the **acromioclavicular joint**, or the **biceps tendon**. Referred pain from the cervical spine can also mimic shoulder problems.

Accurate diagnosis is critical because treatment for a rotator cuff tear differs substantially from treatment for frozen shoulder or bursitis. Imaging studies, physical examination, and sometimes diagnostic injections help us isolate the true pain generator.

## Common Causes of Shoulder Pain

### Rotator Cuff Injury

The rotator cuff is a group of four tendons that stabilize the shoulder joint. Partial or complete tears can result from acute trauma or gradual wear. **Rotator cuff injuries** are the most common cause of shoulder pain in adults over 40. Symptoms include weakness, pain with overhead movement, and nighttime discomfort.

### Bursitis and Impingement

The subacromial bursa reduces friction between the rotator cuff and the bone above it. Repetitive overhead motion or poor posture can inflame this tissue, creating **shoulder impingement syndrome**. Pain is typically worst when lifting the arm between 60 and 120 degrees.

### Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder)

The joint capsule thickens and tightens, severely restricting movement. **Frozen shoulder** progresses through freezing, frozen, and thawing stages over months to years. It is more common in women, diabetics, and patients recovering from shoulder surgery or prolonged immobilization.

### Labral Tears

The labrum is a ring of cartilage that deepens the shoulder socket. Tears can result from falls, direct impacts, or repetitive overhead throwing motions. **SLAP tears** and Bankart lesions produce catching, locking, or a sense of instability in the joint.

### Osteoarthritis of the Shoulder

Cartilage loss in the glenohumeral joint causes pain, stiffness, and grinding sensations. **Shoulder arthritis** develops gradually and worsens over time. It is especially common in patients with a history of prior shoulder injury.

### Referred Pain from the Cervical Spine

Pinched nerves in the neck can produce pain that radiates into the shoulder. This pattern can be confusing because the shoulder examination may appear relatively normal. A thorough evaluation distinguishes true shoulder pathology from cervical referred pain.

## Common Symptoms of Shoulder Pain

- Deep aching in the shoulder joint that worsens with activity
- Sharp pain when reaching overhead or behind the back
- **Nighttime pain** that disrupts sleep, especially when lying on the affected side
- Weakness when lifting or rotating the arm
- Stiffness and reduced range of motion
- Grinding, clicking, or popping sensations during movement
- Swelling or tenderness around the shoulder
- Pain radiating down the upper arm toward the elbow
- Difficulty performing daily tasks like dressing, driving, or carrying objects
- A sensation of the shoulder catching, locking, or giving way

## How We Effectively Treat Shoulder Pain in St. Louis

**Shoulder pain treatment depends entirely on the specific structure causing the problem.** A rotator cuff tear, a frozen shoulder, and bursitis all produce similar symptoms. They require different treatments. Our evaluation process identifies the pain source before any procedure is recommended.

Most shoulder conditions respond well to non-surgical interventional care. We prioritize treatments that promote tissue healing and restore function without the risks and recovery time of surgery.

### PRP Therapy

[Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy](/orthopedic/prp-therapy/) uses concentrated growth factors from your own blood to accelerate tissue repair. For **rotator cuff injuries**, partial tears, and tendinopathy, PRP injections deliver healing proteins directly to the damaged area. This regenerative approach supports natural recovery and can delay or eliminate the need for surgical intervention.

### Injection Therapy

[Targeted injection therapy](/pain-control/injection-therapy/) serves both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. **Corticosteroid injections** reduce inflammation in the bursa or joint capsule. Diagnostic injections with local anesthetic help confirm the exact pain source. Hyaluronic acid injections provide lubrication and cushioning for arthritic shoulders.

### Sports Medicine Rehabilitation

Our [sports medicine](/sports-medicine/) approach combines structured rehabilitation with performance-focused recovery. Strengthening the rotator cuff, improving scapular mechanics, and restoring proper movement patterns are essential for lasting shoulder pain relief. This is particularly important for active patients and those returning to sports or physical occupations.

## Shoulder Pain FAQs for St. Louis Patients

**How do I know if my shoulder pain is serious?**
Pain that persists for more than two weeks, wakes you at night, or limits your ability to use the arm warrants professional evaluation. Sudden weakness or inability to raise the arm suggests a possible rotator cuff tear and should be assessed promptly.

**Do I need an MRI for shoulder pain?**
An MRI is often recommended to evaluate soft tissue structures like the rotator cuff, labrum, and bursa. X-rays can identify arthritis or bone spurs. We determine the right imaging based on your symptoms and examination findings.

**Can shoulder pain be treated without surgery?**
Many shoulder conditions respond excellently to non-surgical treatments. PRP therapy, targeted injections, and structured rehabilitation resolve pain for the majority of patients we see. Surgery is considered only when conservative measures have been thoroughly explored.

**How long does shoulder pain treatment take to work?**
Timeline depends on the diagnosis. Injection therapy may provide relief within days. PRP therapy typically shows improvement over four to six weeks as tissue healing progresses. Rehabilitation programs usually run eight to twelve weeks.

**Is my shoulder pain coming from my neck?**
It is possible. Cervical disc herniations and pinched nerves can refer pain into the shoulder. Our evaluation process includes cervical spine screening to rule out this pattern. Treating the wrong structure wastes time and prolongs suffering.
