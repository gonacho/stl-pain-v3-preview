## Physical Therapy vs. Surgery for Back Pain: The Conservative-First Approach

Surgery should be the last option for [back pain](/conditions/back-pain/), not the first. That statement is not just our opinion. It aligns with guidelines from the American College of Physicians, which recommends exhausting non-surgical treatments before considering surgical intervention for most back pain conditions.

The reality is that 85% to 90% of back pain episodes resolve without surgery. **Physical therapy** strengthens the structures supporting your spine, corrects movement patterns that cause pain, and restores function without the risks of surgical intervention.

St. Louis Pain Center follows a conservative-first treatment philosophy. We combine physical therapy with targeted procedures like [nerve blocks](/pain-control/nerve-blocks/) and [kyphoplasty](/pain-control/kyphoplasty/) to treat back pain at its source.

## How Physical Therapy Treats Back Pain

**Physical therapy** for back pain involves structured exercise programs, manual therapy techniques, and movement re-education designed to:

- Strengthen core muscles that stabilize the spine
- Improve flexibility in the hips, hamstrings, and thoracic spine
- Correct posture and ergonomic habits that worsen pain
- Reduce nerve compression through targeted stretching
- Build endurance for daily activities

A typical physical therapy program for back pain runs 6 to 12 weeks with 2 to 3 sessions per week. Most patients notice meaningful improvement within the first 2 to 4 weeks.

## When Surgery Becomes Necessary

Some back pain conditions do require surgical intervention. These include:

- **Cauda equina syndrome** (emergency, loss of bladder/bowel control)
- **Progressive neurological deficits** (worsening leg weakness)
- **Structural instability** that cannot be stabilized conservatively
- **Failed conservative treatment** after 6 or more months of consistent therapy

Even in surgical candidates, pre-surgical physical therapy (prehabilitation) improves outcomes and speeds post-surgical recovery.

## The St. Louis Pain Center Approach

Our conservative-first protocol for back pain follows a stepped model:

### Step 1: Diagnosis and Physical Therapy
Identify the pain source through examination and imaging. Begin targeted physical therapy.

### Step 2: Add Targeted Procedures if Needed
If physical therapy alone does not resolve the pain, add [nerve blocks](/pain-control/nerve-blocks/) for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. [Injection therapy](/pain-control/injection-therapy/) targets specific inflammatory sites.

### Step 3: Minimally Invasive Procedures
For compression fractures, [kyphoplasty](/pain-control/kyphoplasty/) restores vertebral height and eliminates fracture pain in a 20-minute outpatient procedure. This avoids the risks and recovery time of open spinal surgery.

### Step 4: Surgical Referral (When Necessary)
If conservative treatments have been exhausted and structural pathology requires correction, we coordinate surgical referrals with the appropriate specialists.

The Midwest desk-job culture puts St. Louis residents at particular risk for back pain. Prolonged sitting, combined with cold-weather inactivity, weakens the spinal support structures that physical therapy rebuilds.

## Physical Therapy vs. Surgery FAQs

### How long should I try physical therapy before considering surgery?
Most guidelines recommend 6 to 12 weeks of consistent physical therapy before pursuing surgical options. Some conditions respond within the first few sessions.

### Is physical therapy painful?
You may experience muscle soreness during the first week as your body adapts to new exercises. This is normal and different from the back pain being treated. Your therapist adjusts intensity based on your tolerance.

### Can physical therapy help after back surgery?
Yes. Post-surgical rehabilitation through physical therapy is essential for full recovery. Our team creates recovery protocols for patients returning from spinal procedures.

### What if physical therapy does not work for my back pain?
We offer multiple interventional options including nerve blocks, injection therapy, and kyphoplasty. Your care team adjusts the treatment plan based on your response at each stage.
