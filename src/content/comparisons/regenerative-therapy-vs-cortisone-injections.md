## Regenerative Therapy vs. Cortisone Injections: Understanding the Difference

Cortisone injections have been the standard treatment for joint inflammation for decades. They work. They reduce swelling and pain quickly. They also come with significant limitations that most patients never hear about until the injections stop working.

**Regenerative therapy** represents a fundamentally different approach. Rather than suppressing inflammation, regenerative treatments stimulate your body's repair mechanisms to rebuild damaged tissue. St. Louis Pain Center favors a regenerative-first strategy for patients who want lasting improvement rather than temporary relief.

## How Cortisone Injections Work

Cortisone (corticosteroid) injections deliver a powerful anti-inflammatory directly into the joint or soft tissue. Pain relief typically begins within 48 to 72 hours. A single injection can provide 6 to 12 weeks of reduced pain and swelling.

### The Limitation of Cortisone

Cortisone does not repair tissue. It masks the inflammatory response while the underlying damage continues. Research published in the journal Radiology found that repeated cortisone injections accelerated cartilage loss in arthritic knees. Most physicians now limit cortisone to 3 to 4 injections per joint per year.

For patients with progressive [arthritis](/conditions/arthritis-osteoarthritis/) or significant cartilage loss, cortisone becomes a diminishing-returns cycle. Each injection works for a shorter period. The joint continues to deteriorate.

## How Regenerative Therapy Works

**Regenerative therapy** activates your body's healing pathways to repair damaged tissue at the cellular level. Multiple regenerative approaches exist. St. Louis Pain Center uses protocols tailored to each patient's joint condition, tissue type, and recovery goals.

Regenerative treatments target the damaged cells and structures directly. Rather than turning off the pain signal, they address what is causing the pain in the first place.

### Why Regenerative-First Makes Sense

Starting with regenerative therapy preserves options. Cortisone can always be added later for acute flare-ups. Starting with cortisone and delaying regenerative care means treating joints that have sustained additional damage during the waiting period.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Factor | Regenerative Therapy | Cortisone Injections |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Goal** | Repair damaged tissue | Reduce inflammation |
| **Mechanism** | Stimulates cell repair | Suppresses immune response |
| **Onset** | Gradual (2-6 weeks) | Fast (48-72 hours) |
| **Duration** | Months to years | 6-12 weeks |
| **Repeat limit** | No strict limit | 3-4 per joint per year |
| **Cartilage effect** | Promotes repair | May accelerate loss |
| **Best for** | Chronic conditions, long-term | Acute flare-ups, short-term |

## When Cortisone Still Makes Sense

Cortisone is not the wrong choice in every situation. Acute inflammatory episodes, severe pain requiring immediate function restoration, and pre-surgical pain management all have legitimate uses for cortisone. The concern arises when cortisone becomes the default long-term strategy for progressive conditions.

## When to Choose Regenerative Therapy

Patients with [joint pain](/conditions/joint-pain/), [knee pain](/conditions/knee-pain/), or [arthritis](/conditions/arthritis-osteoarthritis/) who want to address the source of their pain rather than suppress symptoms are strong candidates for regenerative therapy. Younger patients and those hoping to delay or avoid joint replacement surgery benefit most from early regenerative intervention.
